# Wheat value-added products — pasta, biscuits, cereals: the surplus-to-shelf opportunity

*The wheat analogue of the byproduct-import-substitution thesis: India is wheat-surplus, sells
it cheap through OMSS, yet imports premium pasta, specialty biscuits, breakfast cereals — and
the durum feedstock pasta needs. July 2026. Companion to
[byproduct-product-tree.md](byproduct-product-tree.md) and
[consolidated-savings-by-crop.md](consolidated-savings-by-crop.md).*

## The setup: abundant, cheap wheat feedstock

India procures ~300 LMT of wheat a year, holds central-pool stocks at ~2× buffer norm, and
sells it to flour millers through **OMSS at ~₹2,550/qtl** — a large, cheap, assured feedstock.
The question this report asks: is India capturing the *value* of that wheat, or shipping it as
flour while importing the finished products made from it?

**What the exact data shows — three sharp facts:**
1. **Biscuits are India's strength** (₹686 cr export vs ₹137 cr import) — the mass tier exports,
   but **premium biscuit imports are growing +28%**.
2. **Pasta is nascent both ways** — India net-exports only **₹63 cr (~$7.5 mn)** and imports
   **₹53 cr, growing +24%**. Tiny in a multi-billion-dollar global trade — the opportunity, not
   the ceiling.
3. **Wheat gluten is the clean import gap** — imports **₹26.8 cr, growing +35%**, exports
   essentially nil. And India *already exports* semolina (₹43 cr) — the pasta feedstock is
   leaving the country rather than being made into pasta at home.

## The domestic market it feeds — large, and for pasta fast-growing

*A >US$11 bn domestic market for wheat-processed products, all sitting on India's own surplus wheat.*

## The honest picture — this is NOT the RBO story

Unlike rice bran oil (where India imports the derivative wholesale), wheat products are more
nuanced, and overstating it would be wrong:

- **India is already a biscuit powerhouse.** Parle-G is the world's largest-selling biscuit;
  India exports biscuits to 180+ countries (US ~37%, UAE ~12% of shipments). Mass-market
  bakery and biscuits are a domestic *strength*, not an import gap.
- So the opportunity is **not** "India imports its biscuits" — it is four sharper, real gaps.

## The four real opportunities

1. **Pasta — the fast-growing prize with a feedstock gap.** The pasta market is growing
   ~14–16%/yr (the fastest wheat-product segment), and India still imports premium pasta from
   Italy *and* — critically — **the durum wheat/semolina it is made from**. India is the
   **world's #2 durum-semolina importer** (~1,737 shipments), because pasta needs *durum* (hard,
   high-protein) wheat, while India grows mostly soft/medium wheat. Domestic durum is limited
   (MP's "Malwa"/sharbati belt, ~1.5–2 MT). **The double play: expand durum cultivation (an
   MSV/procurement lever for a specific wheat variety) + mill semolina from it + make pasta —
   substituting both the pasta imports and the durum imports.** Import substitution is already
   visible (durum-pasta imports fell −25% YoY) — the trend is the proof.

2. **Premium / value-added biscuits & cereals.** Behind the 30–35% cereal-import tariff and in
   the premium biscuit tier, imports persist. India's mass strength doesn't extend to the
   high-value, health-positioned, or specialty segments — the same "commodity strong, molecular
   tail imported" pattern the byproduct tree found everywhere.

3. **Vital wheat gluten — the ingredient India imports while holding the wheat.** ~13,000 t/yr
   imported (China 88%), the wet-milling co-product that goes into bakery, meat-analogue, and
   pet food ([byproduct-product-tree.md](byproduct-product-tree.md)). A wheat-starch + gluten
   plant on OMSS wheat substitutes this directly.

4. **Export scale-up on cheap feedstock.** India already exports biscuits and some pasta;
   surplus OMSS wheat is a feedstock-cost advantage no import-dependent competitor has. The
   wheat surplus that is a *fiscal drag* as buffer stock is an *export input* as processed
   product — turning a storage cost into an export earner (detailed below).

## The pasta export opportunity — tiny today, a multi-billion-dollar market

India's pasta *export* is nascent — and that is the opportunity, not the limit:

| | Value | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **India pasta exports (HS 1902, DGCI&S)** | **₹63 cr / ~$7.5 mn** (FY25-26) | net exporter, but only just — imports ₹53 cr, growing +24% |
| Top destinations | Bhutan, US, UAE | neighbours + diaspora, not yet the mass import markets |
| **Global pasta imports (the prize)** | **US $1.62 bn · Germany $1.24 bn · France $1.08 bn** | India's ~$7.5 mn is **under 0.5% of the US market alone** |
| By volume (2024) | Germany 467k t · US 302k t · UK 283k t · Japan 70k t | Italy is the dominant exporter (the incumbent to take share from) |

The gap is even starker on the official DGCI&S numbers than the industry estimates suggest:
India ships **~$7.5 mn** of pasta into a global import trade of **billions per major market** —
while it *exports* the semolina feedstock (₹43 cr) that could instead be milled into that pasta
at home.

**The asymmetry:** India ships ~$46 mn of pasta into a global import trade worth *billions* per
major market, dominated by Italy. India's edge is the one Italy lacks — **cheap surplus wheat**
(OMSS ~₹2,550/qtl) as feedstock. Italy's edge is durum quality + brand. So the realistic export
ladder is:

1. **Commodity / mid-market pasta to price-sensitive geographies** (Africa, Middle East, South &
   SE Asia) on the OMSS-wheat cost advantage — where price beats brand.
2. **Diaspora & ethnic-retail** (US, UAE, UK) — already the current export base, scalable.
3. **Premium durum pasta** — gated by domestic durum quality, so it *follows* the
   durum-MSV production lever, not precedes it.

**Why it fits the thesis:** the wheat surplus that costs the exchequer to store becomes an
export earner as pasta — the same "process the surplus, contract the offtake, capture the
margin" logic, with the export channel as the offtake and cheap OMSS wheat as the moat. Scaling
from $46 mn toward even 1–2% of the global pasta import trade is a multi-hundred-million-dollar
export line built on grain India is *paying to hold*.

**Caveat:** commodity-pasta export margins are thin and Italy/Turkey are entrenched low-cost
competitors too; the durum-quality constraint caps the premium tier until domestic durum scales;
and export competitiveness depends on wheat *not* being export-banned (the 2022–24 wheat-product
export flows were disrupted by the grain ban — a policy risk the processed-export case must price
in).

## The value ladder — from OMSS wheat to the shelf

```
OMSS wheat ₹25.5/kg → maida/semolina → PASTA (₹120-300/kg premium) ── imports substituted
                                     → BISCUITS (₹80-200/kg)        ── export scale-up
                                     → BREAKFAST CEREAL (₹200-500/kg) ── premium tier
   wheat → wet-mill → VITAL GLUTEN (₹108/kg) + STARCH               ── 13,000 t/yr import cut
   + BRAN → feed / dietary fibre
```

The processing margin from ₹25.5/kg wheat to ₹120–300/kg pasta or ₹200–500/kg cereal is exactly
the value the state forgoes when it sells wheat raw through OMSS. The same three design rules
apply as everywhere in this project: **use the surplus feedstock (OMSS wheat), capture the
processing margin (mill semolina/gluten, don't sell raw), and contract the offtake** (food
processors, the export channel, and — for durum — a variety-specific procurement guarantee that
gives farmers a reason to grow it).

## Recommendations

1. **A durum-wheat MSV/procurement track** — a variety-specific guarantee (MP + expansion) that
   de-risks durum cultivation, closing the #2-importer durum gap and feeding a domestic pasta
   industry. This is the wheat parallel to the oilseed-MSP case: procurement as a
   *production-catalyst* for a variety India under-grows.
2. **Semolina/pasta and gluten-starch units on OMSS wheat** — sited in the operational Mega
   Food Parks, financed under PMKSY/PLISFPI, the wheat analogue of the rice biorefinery.
3. **Premium and export tiers, not mass** — India's mass biscuit/bakery base is strong; target
   the imported premium segments and the export scale-up, where the margin and the gap are.

## Caveats

Trade figures are DGCI&S TradeStat EIDB commodity-wise, exact 8-digit HS, FY2024-25 and
FY2025-26 provisional (user-supplied primary exports; [`data/wheat_products_trade_dgcis.csv`](../data/wheat_products_trade_dgcis.csv)),
cross-referenced with the `india-trade-tracker` chapter warehouse (TIA Portal + PIB validated)
and located via the graphify link to the cross-repo *Import Substitution Opportunity Matrix* in
`textile_data/research`. FY25-26 is provisional (partial year, subject to revision). Pasta
₹63 cr ≈ $7.5 mn at ~₹84/$; broader trade-database estimates (e.g. Volza ~$46 mn) use different
aggregation/shipment-value bases — the DGCI&S HS-1902 figure is the authoritative one.
Market sizes are industry-research figures (2025, multiple sources — pasta $1.21 bn; biscuits
$5.0 bn; cereals $5.14 bn); growth CAGRs vary 14–16% (pasta) across sources. Durum-pasta import
shipment counts (Volza) are directional, not customs-value totals. India's durum production (~1.5–2 MT,
MP) is an order-of-magnitude estimate. Biscuit *export* leadership is real; the *premium-import*
gap is smaller than the mass market and should not be overstated. This is an opportunity map,
not a demand forecast — and the durum-cultivation lever depends on agronomy (durum suits
specific agro-climatic zones, not all wheat belts).
