# Executive summary — The Grain Value Loop: from MSP procurement to domestic edible oil

*One-document synthesis of the project's edible-oil, PDS, fiscal, and national-build-out work. The
thesis in a line: **India procures the crops that make its cooking oil, gives the byproducts away, and
imports the oil — a ₹1.68 lakh-cr habit it owns the feedstock to break.** Every figure traces to a
verified source (DGCI&S, DFPD, PIB/FCI, SOPA, CCI, SEA, SFAC). July 2026. Front door to the ~12 detail
reports linked throughout.*

## Bottom line — India pays *twice* for imports it then subsidises

The sharpest way to see the waste: several products India **imports** are then **sold to consumers at a
subsidy** — so the country pays twice for the same good, and both payments leave value offshore.

| Product | (1) Import bill | (2) …then subsidised in PDS | Both payments fund |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Edible oil** | **₹1.68 lakh cr** (168 LMT) | state PDS oil — TN ₹3,800 cr (oil+dal), + Karnataka/Gujarat/AP/Kerala; palmolein sold ₹25/L on ~₹65/L subsidy | **imported** palmolein |
| **Pulses** | **~₹31,000 cr** (6.7 MT) | NAFED subsidised pulses to **11+ states** | imports + some domestic |

**The double cost:** the state spends forex to *import* the oil, then spends budget to *subsidise the
imported oil* to ration-card holders. Domestic substitution cuts **both** — it removes the import and
redirects the subsidy from foreign crushers to Indian farmers and mills.

**The bottom-line savings from making these products at home:**

| Horizon | Saving to the country | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **Now (high-confidence)** | **~₹11,000 cr/yr** | byproduct oils (rice bran + cottonseed) — cheaper per kg, on feedstock the state already procures |
| **As processing scales** | **~₹21,000 cr/yr** | full import-substitution across oils, pulses, starch, gluten, silica |
| **Production-lever ceiling (5–7 yr)** | **~₹84,000 cr** | closing half the 168-LMT oil gap via oilseed output growth |
| **Plus** | the entire state PDS-oil subsidy | shifts from *imported* palmolein to *domestic* RBO — same rupee, spent at home |

So the bottom line is not one number but a ladder: **₹11,000 cr bankable now, ~₹21,000 cr as processing
scales, up to ~₹84,000 cr of imports replaceable over 5–7 years — and every rupee of PDS-oil subsidy
redirected from imports to domestic jobs** — all **without cutting a single beneficiary's entitlement.**

## 1. The problem, sized

India imports **168 LMT of edible oil — US$19.3 bn, ₹1.68 lakh crore** (57% of demand), plus ~₹31,000 cr
of pulses — **~₹2.0 lakh cr/yr for goods the MSP crop basket could produce**
([msp-crops-refined-oil-source.md](msp-crops-refined-oil-source.md), DGCI&S TradeStat / DFPD / SEA-verified).
Palm alone is $9.1 bn / 82 LMT, landing at ~₹96/kg. This is the number every domestic-source claim must beat.

## 2. The MSP crops *are* the oil-feedstock base

The 24 MSP crops span India's entire domestic oil supply
([`data/domestic_oil_feedstock_sources.csv`](../data/domestic_oil_feedstock_sources.csv)): 7 oilseeds
directly (mustard 42 LMT oil, groundnut 12, soybean ~22), plus three **byproduct oils from crops the
state already procures or grows** — rice bran (10 LMT), cottonseed (13.4 LMT), coconut (5 LMT). The
feedstock question isn't "can India grow oil crops" — it does — it's **"is the state capturing that oil,
or importing the shortfall while giving the byproduct away?"**

## 3. The honest verdict — three tiers, not a slogan

| Tier | Streams | Cheaper than ₹96/kg palm? |
|---|---|---|
| **A — byproduct oils** | **rice bran, cottonseed** | **Yes, per-kg** — feedstock cost ~0; RBO ₹60 vs ₹105 parity |
| **B — soft-oil crush** | soybean, sunflower | At parity — cheaper only at *system* level (forex + cake + strategic) |
| **C — premium oilseeds** | mustard, groundnut | No — premium oils; a *production* lever, not a price lever |

The strict answer to "can procuring MSP crops give cheaper oil than importing": **yes at Tier A**
(byproduct oils), **yes for the system at Tier B**, and **as a 5–7-yr production lever at Tier C** —
never a promise of cheaper cooking oil next year. Overclaiming Tier C would discredit the case.

## 4. The two byproduct-oil cases the state already touches

This is the sharpest, most actionable finding — **two domestic oils where the state already procures the
parent crop**, exactly parallel to each other:

| | Rice bran oil | Cottonseed oil |
|---|---|---|
| Parent crop | Paddy | Cotton |
| State procurer | **FCI** (CMR paddy) | **CCI** (7.3M bales at MSP, MY24-25) |
| Byproduct | ~65 LMT bran | ~95–100 LMT cottonseed (from ~5 MMT lint) |
| Oil today | **~10 LMT** (India #1) | **~13.4 LMT** (largest secondary-source oil) |
| Status | bran given to millers **free** | cottonseed sold by ginners, unaggregated |
| Co-products | DORB feed, γ-oryzanol, tocotrienols | cottonseed meal (~45 LMT feed), linters, hull |

**Both are a "rice-bran story": the state procures the crop, the oil rides free on the byproduct.** For
rice bran the fix is to stop giving the bran away (₹7,650 cr/yr); for cottonseed it is to **aggregate the
cottonseed CCI's cotton throws off and crush it** — a cotton-side parallel nobody runs today.

### Ways to utilise cottonseed for oil (the new lever)

Cottonseed is India's biggest *secondary* oil source yet is un-aggregated. Concrete pathways
([`data/domestic_oil_feedstock_sources.csv`](../data/domestic_oil_feedstock_sources.csv); USDA Cotton
Annual 2025, CAI, SOPA):

1. **Link CCI cotton to cottonseed crush.** CCI is the cotton-FCI — it procured **7.3 M bales (~1.6 MMT
   lint) at MSP**, whose cottonseed (~3 MMT) yields **~4 LMT of oil** the state currently lets ginners
   sell loose. A CCI→HVOC cottonseed-crush linkage captures it — the exact analogue of FCI→bran→RBO.
2. **Solvent-extract + refine out gossypol.** Gossypol (cotton's toxic pigment) is the historical barrier;
   modern solvent extraction + refining/deodorising already makes 13.4 LMT food-grade (used in blends,
   vanaspati) — scaling refining capacity upgrades it from industrial to branded cooking-oil tier.
3. **Integrated ginning-crushing units in the cotton belt** (Gujarat, Maharashtra, Telangana, north
   Punjab/Haryana) — co-locate crush with ginning so cottonseed never leaves the cluster loose.
4. **Bank the meal + linters.** ~45 LMT of cottonseed meal (protein feed) and cotton linters (cellulose/
   pharma) make the crush economics work — the co-product credit that pulls cottonseed oil below parity.
5. **FPO/cooperative-run crush** in cotton-belt states, fed by CCI/FPO-aggregated seed — farmer-owned
   value capture on a byproduct that today leaks to unorganised trade.

Soybean is the Tier-B scale case: SOPA puts **123 LMT available for crush (2024-25) → ~22 LMT soy oil**
plus soymeal India *exports* — the cake credit is what makes domestic soy crush competitive.

## 5. The fiscal rationalisation — same rupee, with value capture

| What the system costs now | ₹/yr | What processing recovers | ₹/yr |
|---|---|---|---|
| MSP to ~1.3 cr farmers | ~₹2.32 L cr | Import substitution (processing) | **₹21,087 cr** |
| Food subsidy (free grain, 80 cr) | ₹2.05 L cr | — of which capturable now | ~₹11,000 cr |
| Oil + pulse imports | ~₹2.0 L cr | Byproduct monetised (bran→RBO) | ₹7,650 cr |
| Excess-stock carry (~330 LMT) | ~₹18,000 cr | Processing-margin uplift | 3–5× |

**No double-count** (procurement sits inside the subsidy). The two clean burdens — **₹2.05 L cr subsidy +
~₹2.0 L cr imports** — are what value capture addresses *without cutting the food-security entitlement*:
farmers still get MSP, 80 cr still get free grain; the byproducts stop being gifted and the oil/pulses
stop being imported ([fiscal-rationalisation-procurement-vs-processing.md](fiscal-rationalisation-procurement-vs-processing.md)).

## 6. The distribution rail already exists — PDS + ONORC

The state spends **₹2.05 L cr** and runs **5.43 lakh Fair Price Shops** to feed **80 cr** people — and
where it distributes PDS oil (Tamil Nadu ₹3,800 cr, palmolein at ₹25/L), it subsidises an **import**.
**ONORC** unifies this into one portable, deduplicated national offtake — the captive demand a domestic
PDS-RBO would ride, redirecting the oil subsidy from imported palmolein to domestic jobs
([msp-crops-refined-oil-source.md](msp-crops-refined-oil-source.md)).

## 7. The national build-out — where, and who runs it

**Where:** FCI storage and FPO density sit in *different* states — the storage belt (Punjab 175 LMT, MP
200, Haryana 100) is thin on FPOs; the FPO belt (UP 758, Bihar 312, AP 247) holds less grain. **MP is the
one state with both** → the natural first hub; site oilseed/pulse crush in FPO-dense deficit states and
rice-bran in the paddy belt ([national-fci-fpo-food-processing-psu.md](national-fci-fpo-food-processing-psu.md)).

**Who:** **Don't create a food-processing PSU — revive the one DFPD already owns.** Its org chart holds
**FCI** (grain), **CWC** (storage) and **HVOC — Hindustan Vegetable Oils Corporation**, a dormant
edible-oils CPSE. Build it like the *survivors* (infrastructure + processing, FPO-fed, ring-fenced
offtake) not the *failures* (STC→PEC→NCEL, which traded naked). Ride the **cooperative grid** the Ministry
of Cooperation is already scaling — **857,854 cooperatives, PACS in 83% of gram panchayats**, NCOL/NCEL/
NAFED + the World's Largest Grain Storage Plan — the AMUL-model farmer-owned layer that actually endures.

## 8. What to do — the five moves

1. **Stop the bran giveaway** (₹7,650 cr) — the fastest, highest-confidence Tier-A win; needs no acreage.
2. **Aggregate CCI cottonseed into crush** — the untapped second byproduct-oil (~13 LMT base, ~4 LMT from
   state-procured cotton alone).
3. **Redirect the import spend, not the subsidy** — target the ~₹2 L cr oil/pulse imports; protect the
   ₹2.05 L cr food entitlement.
4. **Revive HVOC** as a JV-capitalised infrastructure+processing CPSE, wired to FCI/CWC/FPOs/PACS.
5. **Pilot in Madhya Pradesh** — the one state with storage + FPOs + oilseed feedstock.
