# Evaluating the BPCL/OMC "40% FCI-rice" ethanol clause — surplus offtake, done conditionally

*The proposal — an FCI clause guaranteeing a minimum 40% of grain-based ethanol from FCI rice, only when
grain is in surplus — is **not hypothetical. It is the live ESY 2025-26 OMC ethanol tender.** This note
evaluates the actual clause: what it does, its safeguards, its economics, and how it fits the
fuel-vs-food and surplus-disposal argument. July 2026. Data:
[`data/ethanol_fci_40pct_clause.csv`](../data/ethanol_fci_40pct_clause.csv). Companion to
[fuel-vs-food-security.md](fuel-vs-food-security.md) and
[omss-ethanol-rbo-import-substitution.md](omss-ethanol-rbo-import-substitution.md).*

## What the clause actually says (ESY 2025-26 tender, BPCL for the OMCs)

The Oil Marketing Companies' tender for **~1,050 crore litres** of ethanol (ESY 2025-26, Tender 22376,
23-Sep-2025) contains, for the first time, a **mandatory FCI-rice floor** on grain-based ethanol:

> *"The quantity offered from 'surplus rice sourced from FCI' should be **minimum 40%** of the total
> quantity offered from grain-based feedstock(s)"* — i.e. **A ≥ 0.40 × (A + B + C)**, where A = FCI surplus
> rice, B = damaged food grains, C = maize (Annexure-I). A bid failing this check *cannot be submitted.*

The "**if there is sufficient grain**" condition is built in as three explicit safeguards:

| Safeguard | Value | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Annual cap** | 52 LMT of FCI rice for ethanol (1-Nov-25 → 30-Jun-26) | ≈ **234 cr L** ethanol (~22% of the 1,050 cr L programme) |
| **Sunset** | FCI-rice allocation valid only to **30-Jun-2026** | forces re-decision before the lean pre-harvest window |
| **Q4 exclusion** | **FCI rice barred in Q4 (Aug-Oct 2026)** | protects stocks in the pre-*kharif* low-stock quarter |

**This is new.** The prior **ESY 2024-25** tender had *no* 40% FCI-rice floor — bidders simply quoted per
feedstock. So ESY 2025-26 is a deliberate policy shift to **guarantee FCI's surplus rice a floor share of
grain ethanol.**

## The economics — a good deal, with one concession

| Feedstock | Ethanol rate (ESY 25-26) | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Surplus rice from FCI** | **₹60.32/L** (rice at ₹2,320/qtl ex-godown) | **cheapest grain feedstock** |
| Damaged food grains | ₹64.00/L | |
| Maize | ₹66.07/L + ₹5.79 incentive | dearest — the diversification feedstock |
| Sugarcane (C-heavy→juice) | ₹57.97 – ₹65.61/L | |

FCI rice is priced into ethanol at **₹2,320/qtl (₹23.20/kg)** — up ₹70 on last year, but still **~₹5.8/kg
(20%) below the ~₹2,900/qtl OMSS rice reserve.** So the clause sells surplus rice to distillers *cheaper
than it sells the same rice to millers* through OMSS. That is the concession: **a below-OMSS price is an
implicit subsidy to energy security**, justified by (a) surplus disposal, (b) forex saved on crude, (c)
feedstock certainty that pulls private grain-ethanol capacity investment.

## Evaluation — where it is right, and where it is blunt

**What the clause gets right:**
1. **It solves the surplus-disposal problem.** The clause converts FCI's above-norm surplus — the same
   idle ~298 LMT "static base" the [utilisation report](warehouse-utilisation-fifo-lifo.md) identifies —
   into a *guaranteed* offtake at ₹2,320/qtl, better than paying carry to store it. Demand-security for
   the surplus is exactly what OMSS never offered.
2. **The safeguards are the right instinct.** The 52-LMT cap + 30-Jun sunset + Q4 exclusion are a crude
   but real *stock-threshold discipline* — the institutionalised version of the [July-2023 FCI-rice-to-
   ethanol pause](fuel-vs-food-security.md). Food is protected in the lean quarter; fuel gets the surplus.
3. **It de-risks distiller investment.** A guaranteed 40% floor gives grain-ethanol plants feedstock
   certainty — the same mandate-creates-offtake logic that took blending from 1.5% to 20%.

**Where it is blunt (the honest critique):**
1. **A *mandate* is not a *threshold*.** The 40% floor is a fixed rule and the "sufficiency" test is a
   pre-set 52-LMT annual cap — **not a dynamic, stock-linked trigger.** If the year turns deficit
   mid-stream (a bad monsoon), the cap is already committed; a genuine rule would key the release to
   *live central-pool stock above buffer+margin* and taper automatically, not to an annual number fixed in
   September.
2. **Below-OMSS pricing.** Selling ethanol-rice at ₹2,320 while OMSS rice is ₹2,900 means the state accepts
   ~₹5.8/kg less to send grain to fuel than to food-processing millers — defensible for surplus, but it
   should at least be *OMSS-parity* so fuel doesn't out-compete food-processing for the same grain.
3. **It's still the fuel-vs-food tier.** This is *grain*-to-fuel — the one route that carries the
   [fuel-vs-food tension](fuel-vs-food-security.md). Mandating 40% FCI rice optimises for *distiller
   feedstock security*, not for *best national use of the grain*.

## The project's read — pair the clause with the byproduct route

The clause is a reasonable **surplus-disposal + energy-security** instrument. But it is the *second-best*
lever, and it does not compete with the *first-best* one:

- **The byproduct route has no such tradeoff.** RBO/cottonseed oil use the *byproduct*, not the grain —
  zero food-vs-fuel tension ([fuel-vs-food-security.md](fuel-vs-food-security.md)). And crucially,
  **the two are complementary, not rival:** the FCI rice that goes to ethanol was *milled from paddy* — the
  **bran was already generated at CMR milling and can still yield RBO upstream**, whatever the milled rice
  is later used for. Ethanol takes the starch; RBO takes the bran; they don't compete for the same fraction.
- **So the ideal policy stack is:** (1) capture the bran → RBO first (no tradeoff, ₹6,700–16,500 cr edible-oil
  substitution); (2) send genuinely-surplus milled rice to ethanol under a *stock-threshold* rule (this
  clause, improved); (3) keep the food entitlement ring-fenced throughout.

## Recommendations on the clause

1. **Make "sufficient grain" dynamic, not a fixed cap.** Trigger the FCI-rice-to-ethanol release on
   *live central-pool stock above buffer norm + a stated margin*, auto-tapering as stock falls — the
   Q4-exclusion already gestures at this; generalise it into a monthly stock-linked rule.
2. **Price ethanol-rice at least at OMSS parity** so fuel does not undercut food-processing for the same grain.
3. **Add a bran-value condition.** Where FCI rice is milled for ethanol supply, require the **bran to be
   routed to RBO extraction** (not fed with oil intact) — capturing the no-tradeoff edible-oil value on top.
4. **Keep the 40% floor, but as a *ceiling-on-mandate*.** A 40% *minimum* guarantees FCI offtake; pair it
   with a stock-linked *maximum* so a bumper-surplus year doesn't over-divert in a later deficit.

## Caveats

Figures are from the BPCL/OMC tenders: **ESY 2025-26** (Tender 22376, 23-Sep-2025 — the 40% clause, 52 LMT
cap, ₹2,320/qtl, ₹60.32/L, 1,050 cr L) and **ESY 2024-25** (Tender of 15-May-2025 — no 40% floor, ₹2,250/qtl,
₹58.50/L). The ~234 cr L from 52 LMT rice uses ~450 L/tonne grain-ethanol yield (indicative). The OMSS-rice
comparison uses the 2023-24 ₹2,900/qtl reserve — OMSS reserve prices vary by year and policy objective, so
the ~₹5.8/kg gap is directional. "Surplus rice from FCI" is the tender's term; the underlying stock
sufficiency is a DFPD/FCI determination this note does not second-guess. Ethanol rates are administered
(Govt/PIB). This is an evaluation of a public tender clause — research synthesis, **not** investment, bidding,
or fiscal advice, and not a recommendation to any bidder.
