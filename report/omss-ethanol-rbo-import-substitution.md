# OMSS, ethanol, and rice-bran oil — sources, volumes, and the import-substitution savings

*The quantified backbone: what the state already sells through OMSS, what the ethanol mandate already
proves, and how the same logic applied to rice-bran oil substitutes edible-oil imports — every volume
sourced. July 2026. Data: [`data/omss_ethanol_rbo_volumes.csv`](../data/omss_ethanol_rbo_volumes.csv),
[`data/rbo_savings_scenarios.py`](../data/rbo_savings_scenarios.py). Consolidates
[rbo-savings-scenarios.md](rbo-savings-scenarios.md), [omss-processing-returns.md](omss-processing-returns.md)
and [msp-crops-refined-oil-source.md](msp-crops-refined-oil-source.md).*

## The import to substitute — the target, sourced

India's edible-oil import bill has grown from **133.52 lakh tonnes / ~₹80,000 crore in 2020-21**
(domestic 122.89 LMT, 47.9% self-sufficient; palm 56%, soy 27%, sunflower 16% — [NMEO-OP Operational
Guidelines, Table 1](https://nmeo.dac.gov.in)) to **168 LMT / ₹1.68 lakh crore in FY2025-26** (DGCI&S
TradeStat, [msp-crops-refined-oil-source.md](msp-crops-refined-oil-source.md)) as volume and price both
rose. That ~₹1.68 lakh cr — 57% import dependence — is the bill any domestic-oil lever aims at. **NMEO's
sibling mission, NMEO-Oilseeds, explicitly names rice bran, cottonseed and coconut as secondary-source
collection pillars** — so the RBO lever below is already stated policy, not a new proposal.

## 1. Ethanol — the proof the model works (mandate → offtake → forex saved)

The ethanol programme is the single best evidence that a **government mandate can create a processing-demand
offtake for surplus/broken grain and convert it into import substitution and forex savings** — exactly the
template the edible-oil case needs. Sourced volumes ([PIB / MoPNG](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2113234);
[NITI Ethanol Roadmap 2020-25](https://www.niti.gov.in/sites/default/files/2021-06/EthanolBlendingInIndia_compressed.pdf)):

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Ethanol production | **38 crore L (2014) → 661.1 crore L (Jun-2025)** |
| Blending rate | 8.17% (2020-21) → 14.6% (2023-24) → **20% (Mar-2025)** — 5 yrs early |
| Feedstock | sugarcane/molasses, **maize, broken/damaged rice** |
| Farmer earnings (to 2025) | **₹1.18 lakh crore** |
| **Forex saved (crude cut)** | **₹1.36 lakh crore** |

**The lesson:** the state *created* this demand by blending mandate and *throttles* it by allocation. It
turned surplus/broken grain into ₹1.36 lakh cr of forex saved. The edible-oil bill (₹1.68 lakh cr) is a
comparable prize — and RBO is the grain-side lever that hasn't been pulled.

## 2. OMSS — the proof the channel exists (grain → processors)

The Open Market Sale Scheme (Domestic) already does what the biorefinery needs — **FCI sells surplus grain
to processors/millers at a published reserve price** ([PIB PRID 2000937](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2000937),
2023-24, till 24-01-2024):

| OMSS(D) 2023-24 | Allocated | Sold | Reserve price |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wheat | 101.5 LMT | **71.01 LMT** | ₹2,150/qtl (FAQ) |
| Rice | 25 LMT | **1.62 LMT** (highest-ever to private) | ₹2,900/qtl |

Buyers are **flour millers and processors**, bidding 10–100 MT lots. Two things follow: (a) the channel
to route government grain into processing is **proven and operational** — 71 LMT of wheat moved to millers
in one year; (b) **OMSS rice → millers → bran is the RBO feedstock pathway** already in motion — the grain
the state sells for milling *generates the bran* the RBO lever needs.

## 3. Rice-bran oil — the lever, its volumes, and the savings

Rice bran is the byproduct of milling paddy (CMR + OMSS rice). India already leads the world in RBO
(~10 LMT) but leaves most bran's oil in cattle feed. The scenario model
([`rbo_savings_scenarios.py`](../data/rbo_savings_scenarios.py); oil yield 16%; **ex-mill ₹60/kg vs import
parity ₹105/kg = ₹45/kg edge**; DORB feed credit ₹15/kg):

| Base | Bran | RBO out | **Import displaced** | Net margin | DORB feed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Govt CMR bran** (~40 LMT, 41% of total) — 100% processed | 39.9 LMT | 6.4 LMT | **₹6,698 cr** | ₹2,871 cr | ₹4,665 cr |
| **All-India bran** (~98 LMT) — 100% processed | 98.0 LMT | 15.7 LMT | **₹16,464 cr** | ₹7,056 cr | ₹11,466 cr |
| **Realistic near-term** — the ~48 LMT bran now sent to feed with oil intact | — | +7.7 LMT | **₹8,064 cr (~5% of the ₹1.68 lakh-cr bill)** | ₹3,456 cr | ₹5,616 cr |

Every **25 percentage points of the government's own CMR bran processed ≈ ₹1,675 cr** of import saved — a
linear, dial-able lever, on feedstock the state already controls and currently gives away (the ₹7,650 cr
CMR-bran giveaway, [omss-processing-returns.md](omss-processing-returns.md)).

## The chain — why the three connect

| Scheme | What it proves | Volume evidence | Read-across to edible oil |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ethanol** | a mandate can build a grain-processing offtake that saves forex | 661 cr L; **₹1.36 lakh cr forex saved** | do the same for oil: an RBO/fortified-oil offtake |
| **OMSS** | the state can channel grain to processors at reserve price | **71 LMT wheat sold** to millers, 2023-24 | OMSS rice → millers → **bran** = RBO feedstock |
| **RBO** | the bran byproduct substitutes imported oil | ~40–98 LMT bran → **6.4–15.7 LMT RBO** | **₹6,698–16,464 cr import displaced** |

**Ethanol proves the model; OMSS proves the channel; RBO is the un-pulled lever** — ₹6,700 cr from the
government's own bran, ₹16,500 cr at the all-India ceiling, against a ₹1.68 lakh-cr import bill NMEO itself
wants cut. The offtake certainty the ethanol mandate created for fuel is exactly what a PDS-oil /
fortification mandate would create for RBO ([msp-crops-refined-oil-source.md](msp-crops-refined-oil-source.md)).

## Caveats

Ethanol figures are PIB/MoPNG (production, blending %, ₹1.36 lakh cr forex — the forex figure is the
programme's cumulative crude-substitution claim, not an audited net). OMSS volumes are PIB PRID 2000937
(2023-24, partial-year to 24-01-2024); reserve prices and sold quantities vary year to year with the
price-control objective. RBO savings are the project's [scenario model](../data/rbo_savings_scenarios.py)
(oil yield 16%, ₹45/kg parity gap, DORB credit) — *potential* import displacement at full processing, not a
realised figure; actual capture depends on extraction capex, bran logistics, and RBO demand
([rbo-savings-scenarios.md](rbo-savings-scenarios.md) Part 2 on demand security). The ₹80,000 cr (2020-21)
and ₹1.68 lakh cr (2025-26) import bills are different years — the growth is real (volume + price), not a
discrepancy. NMEO-OP (oil palm) and NMEO-OS (oilseeds, which covers rice bran/cottonseed) are sibling
missions ([oilseed-msp-case.md](oilseed-msp-case.md)). Research synthesis, not investment or fiscal advice.
