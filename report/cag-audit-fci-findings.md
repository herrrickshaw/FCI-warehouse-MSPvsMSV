# What the CAG found — independent audit validation of the storage-inefficiency case

*The project's critique of FCI storage (over-hiring, under-utilisation, an idle static base, avoidable
carry) is not just this study's inference — the Comptroller & Auditor General of India said the same,
with audited numbers. Summary of **CAG Report No. 20 of 2023, Performance Audit on Storage Management and
Movement of Food grains by FCI** (period 2017-18 to 2021-22, year ended March 2022). July 2026. Data:
[`data/cag_fci_audit_findings.csv`](../data/cag_fci_audit_findings.csv). Validates
[warehouse-utilisation-fifo-lifo.md](warehouse-utilisation-fifo-lifo.md),
[fiscal-rationalisation-procurement-vs-processing.md](fiscal-rationalisation-procurement-vs-processing.md)
and [national-fci-fpo-food-processing-psu.md](national-fci-fpo-food-processing-psu.md).*

## Why this matters

Every efficiency claim in this project — that FCI over-hires instead of building/using own capacity, that
a static base sits idle, that the network is 70% rented, that storage augmentation lagged — is an
*inference* from public stock and capacity data. **The CAG audit confirms each independently, with figures
and paragraph references.** That turns the argument from "a plausible reading" into "the state's own
supreme auditor agrees."

## The audited findings, mapped to the project's claims

| CAG finding | Amount | Para | Project claim it validates |
|---|---:|---|---|
| **Avoidable hiring, Punjab/Haryana** — 10.55 LMT hired despite own vacant CAP | **₹62.76 cr** | 3.4.3 | over-hiring / **utilisation mismatch** — size to average, hire for peak |
| **Avoidable carry-over charges to SGAs** — vacant capacity not used for direct delivery | **₹170.26 cr** | 3.4.6 | the same mismatch; FCI is **70% hired** |
| **Augmentation shortfall** — only **12.25 of 34.50 LMT** target created in 5 yrs | — | 1.1.1 | storage grew slowly (851→918 LMT) — the [scenario](../dashboard/storage-scenario.html) |
| Sangrur silo taken over without despatch facility — **stock blocked** | ₹6.95 cr | 3.6.5.1 | the **idle static base** — grain that doesn't move |
| Short-despatch storage charges, Punjab/Haryana | ₹182.29 cr | 4.3.3 | movement inefficiency / avoidable carry |
| Direct railhead issue foregone — **10% in Jharkhand alone** would save | ₹158.88 cr | 4.3.4 | **distributed delivery cuts cost** — the PACS-node logic |
| **Long-Term Tariff Contract not availed** — railway-freight rebate forgone | **₹1,736.11 cr** | 4.4.1 | the single largest avoidable cost — inefficiency at scale |
| Silo land/contract lapses (Assam, Bihar) + damages not recovered + undue favour | ₹58.78 cr | 3.5.x | weak augmentation execution & contract governance |
| PDS stock **not FSSAI-tested** (stored <6 months / direct railhead) | — | 3.7.4 | a *caveat* to "spoilage negligible" — testing has gaps |

**The quantified avoidable costs above total ~₹2,383 crore over 2017-22, dominated by the ₹1,736 cr
freight-rebate lapse** — and that is only the slice the CAG put a number on. The direction is unambiguous:
FCI leaves large sums on the table in storage and movement.

## How it sharpens each part of the thesis

1. **Utilisation (over-hiring).** The CAG's ₹62.76 cr avoidable hiring + ₹170.26 cr carry-over — *despite
   own vacant CAP* — is the audited version of [the utilisation report's](warehouse-utilisation-fifo-lifo.md)
   "size owned capacity to the average, hire for the peak; stop paying to hire what you already have vacant."
2. **The static base.** The Sangrur blocked-stock finding is a concrete instance of the ~298 LMT base that
   *sits* — reinforcing that the fix is to **route surplus to processing**, not to keep storing it.
3. **Augmentation lagged → the cooperative plan.** Only 12.25 of 34.50 LMT built in five years is exactly
   why the [World's Largest Grain Storage Plan](national-fci-fpo-food-processing-psu.md) shifts capacity to
   the **PACS/cooperative** layer — FCI's own build-out under-delivered.
4. **Distributed delivery.** The ₹158.88 cr Jharkhand railhead-issue finding validates siting storage +
   processing **at the state/PACS level** rather than moving grain the long way round.
5. **Restructuring.** The report also records the **Shanta Kumar High Level Committee (2015)** — which
   recommended reorienting FCI's role in MSP/storage/distribution and direct subsidy — the policy lineage
   for the [HVOC-revival / infrastructure-PSU](national-fci-fpo-food-processing-psu.md) argument.

## The honest counterweight

The CAG is a critique of *execution*, not proof the whole system is broken — FCI still runs the largest
food-security operation on earth with negligible spoilage ([RS UQ-2435](warehouse-utilisation-fifo-lifo.md),
0.002–0.022% non-issuable). Two things are true at once: **the food-security outcome is delivered, and the
storage/movement machinery is materially inefficient.** The project's argument sits precisely there — the
inefficiency (over-hiring, idle base, avoidable freight) is the slack that a processing/value-capture and
cooperative-storage redesign converts to value, *without* touching the entitlement the system delivers well.
The CAG's own FSSAI-testing gap (Para 3.7.4) is also logged honestly — it slightly qualifies the
"spoilage/quality is a solved problem" reading.

## Caveats

Figures are the CAG's audited findings for **2017-18 to 2021-22** (Report No. 20 of 2023) — region-specific
(mostly Punjab/Haryana/Assam/Bihar/Jharkhand/MP), not a national total; the ~₹2,383 cr is the sum of the
*quantified* items in the executive summary, not FCI's full avoidable cost. Audit findings are contested in
the normal ATN (Action Taken Note) process; some may have been remediated since. This note *uses* the CAG
as independent validation of the efficiency critique — it does not reproduce the full report. Research
synthesis; the CAG report is the primary authority. Not investment or fiscal advice.
