# The fiscal rationalisation: what procurement + imports + PDS cost the taxpayer — vs the food-processing option

*A single ledger tying together the project's procurement, holding, subsidy, import and processing
findings. The question: India spends enormously to buy grain from farmers, hold it, and give it away —
while importing the oil and pulses those same MSP crops could yield. What does the whole system cost,
and what could food processing of the MSP crops recover? Grounded in DFPD/PIB procurement bulletins,
FCI stock data, and the project's verified savings work. July 2026. Synthesis of
[holding-economics.md](holding-economics.md), [omss-processing-returns.md](omss-processing-returns.md),
[consolidated-savings-by-crop.md](consolidated-savings-by-crop.md) and
[msp-crops-refined-oil-source.md](msp-crops-refined-oil-source.md).*

## The system in one flow

```
FARMER ──MSP──▶ FCI/state procures ──▶ holds in central pool ──▶ PDS: given FREE to 80 cr
 ~₹2.32 lakh cr      738 LMT stock         ~₹18,000 cr/yr          food subsidy ₹2.05 lakh cr
 to ~1.3 cr farmers  (~330 LMT over norm)  excess-stock carry
                          │
                          └── bran/husk/broken byproducts ──▶ GIVEN AWAY (₹7,650 cr)

MEANWHILE, separately:  IMPORT the oil & pulses the same crops could make ──▶ ~₹2.0 lakh cr/yr
```

The state runs the largest grain-procurement machine on earth for the two crops it does *not* import,
gives away the milling byproducts, and *separately* imports ~₹2 lakh cr of the edible oil and pulses
those very MSP crops could yield. That is the fiscal irrationality the processing option addresses.

## Ledger A — what the system costs the taxpayer/economy

| Block | Line item | ₹ cr/yr | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Procurement** | Paddy MSP paid to farmers (KMS, full season) | ~1,70,000 | ~776 LMT paddy × ₹2,300/qtl; PIB ₹65,695 cr by Dec (partial) |
| | Wheat MSP paid (RMS 2025-26) | **62,156** | 256.31 LMT, 21.03 lakh farmers (PIB/FCI) |
| | **MSP outgo to ~1.3 cr farmers** | **~2,32,000** | paddy + wheat |
| **Holding** | Excess-stock carrying cost | ~18,000 | ~₹5.6/kg/yr × ~330 LMT held over buffer norm |
| **Subsidy** | Central food subsidy FY25 | **2,05,250** | economic cost − ₹0 issue price; free grain to ~80 cr |
| **Imports** | Edible oil | 1,68,000 | 168 LMT; US$19.3 bn |
| | Pulses | ~31,000 | ~6.7 MT; $3.74 bn |
| | **Imports of MSP-crop-derivable goods** | **~1,99,000** | oil + pulses |

**Reading the ledger honestly — avoid the double-count.** Procurement MSP (₹2.32 lakh cr) is *not* a
separate loss on top of the subsidy: most of it is **recovered** as a stock asset and, for PDS-bound
grain, is *inside* the ₹2.05 lakh cr food subsidy (economic cost = MSP + carrying + distribution, minus
the ~₹0 recovery). So the two clean, non-overlapping annual burdens are:

1. **Food subsidy ₹2.05 lakh cr** — the net budgetary cost of procuring, holding and giving grain away.
2. **Imports ~₹2.0 lakh cr** — cash leaving the economy for the oil and pulses the MSP basket could
   produce (of which ~₹0.5 lakh cr returns as customs duty; the rest is forex out).

Plus two avoidable leakages the processing option targets directly: **~₹18,000 cr** of excess-stock
carry and **₹7,650 cr** of milling byproduct given away for free.

## Ledger B — what food processing of the MSP crops recovers

| Recovery stream | ₹ cr/yr | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| **Import substitution via processing** | **21,087** | RBO 7,544 + pulses ~7,000 + cottonseed 1,975 + starch/silica/protein/gluten |
| **Byproduct monetised (not given away)** | **7,650** | ~65 LMT rice bran currently handed to millers free |
| **Processing-margin uplift** | *3–5×* | branded staple processing earns 11–12% EBITDA vs 2–4% raw trading |
| **Excess-stock carry avoided** | up to ~18,000 | processing the surplus draws down the ~330 LMT over-buffer that costs carry |

**The core recoverable number: ~₹21,000 cr/yr of import substitution** ([consolidated-savings-by-crop.md](consolidated-savings-by-crop.md)),
of which ~₹11,000 cr is high-confidence and capturable now ([msp-crops-refined-oil-source.md](msp-crops-refined-oil-source.md)),
plus the ₹7,650 cr byproduct the state currently gifts away. And critically — the money is **in the
processing, not the sourcing**: verified balance sheets show branded staple processors earn 3–5× the
raw trader's margin on the same grain ([omss-processing-returns.md](omss-processing-returns.md)).

## The rationalisation — reframing the same rupee

The status quo and the processing option are not "spend more vs spend less." They are **the same
procurement, holding and distribution spend, with or without value capture on the way through:**

| | Status quo | With MSP-crop food processing |
|---|---|---|
| Procure grain from farmers | ✅ ₹2.32 lakh cr (unchanged) | ✅ ₹2.32 lakh cr (unchanged — farmer income protected) |
| Give grain free to 80 cr | ✅ ₹2.05 lakh cr subsidy | ✅ **unchanged — food-security entitlement untouched** |
| Milling byproducts | ❌ given away (₹7,650 cr) | ✅ **monetised into RBO, feed, silica** |
| Oil & pulses | ❌ imported (~₹2.0 lakh cr) | ✅ **~₹21,000 cr substituted domestically, rising** |
| Excess stock | ❌ carried at ~₹18,000 cr/yr | ✅ **processed down, carry avoided** |
| Jobs | milling only | ✅ **+ crush, refine, food-processing, nutraceutical** |

**Nothing in the food-security guarantee is cut.** The farmer still gets MSP; the beneficiary still gets
free grain. What changes is that the byproducts stop being given away and the derivatives stop being
imported — the surplus and the byproduct are *processed* instead of *dumped and re-imported*. The
processing option is a **value-capture layer bolted onto a machine that already runs at ₹4 lakh cr/yr**,
not a new subsidy.

## Why this is the efficient use of the buffer, not a raid on it

Three guardrails keep this honest:

1. **Buffer first, process the surplus.** India holds ~330 LMT *over* the buffer norm (738 vs ~410 LMT).
   The processing feedstock comes from that surplus and from byproducts (bran/husk) — *not* from the
   food-security buffer, which stays intact ([holding-economics.md](holding-economics.md): the state
   is the only actor for whom holding "works", because it pays the negative carry as policy — so draw
   the *excess* into value, not the norm).
2. **Contract the offtake, don't chase the shelf.** The demand is contractable — fortification mandates,
   PDS/ICDS, food-processor supply agreements, the [ONORC national offtake](msp-crops-refined-oil-source.md) —
   the ethanol-blending-mandate lever applied to processed grain.
3. **Capital is 35–50% subsidised already.** PMFME/PMKSY/PLISFPI fund the processing units, so the
   value-capture layer is built on existing scheme money, not fresh outlay.

## Recommendations

1. **Treat the ~330 LMT over-buffer as processing feedstock, not dead storage** — the ₹18,000 cr/yr
   carry on excess stock is the clearest waste; divert the surplus into OMSS-fed processing.
2. **Stop the byproduct giveaway** — capturing the ₹7,650 cr of free bran (→ RBO) is the single fastest,
   highest-confidence recovery, needing no new acreage.
3. **Redirect the import spend, not the subsidy** — the ~₹2 lakh cr oil/pulse import bill is the target;
   the ₹2.05 lakh cr food subsidy is the protected floor. Substitute imports; don't cut entitlements.
4. **Bank the processing margin domestically** — site branded/derivative processing (11–12% margin)
   inside the operational Mega Food Parks under existing 35–50% capital subsidy.

## Caveats

Procurement MSP outgo is a full-season estimate (paddy ~₹1.7 lakh cr from ~776 LMT paddy × ₹2,300/qtl;
PIB reports ₹65,695 cr paid by 1 Dec 2024, mid-season) plus verified wheat ₹62,156 cr (RMS 2025-26,
PIB/FCI); combined ~₹2.32 lakh cr is indicative. Food subsidy ₹2,05,250 cr is PRS Demand-for-Grants
2024-25. Central pool 738.19 LMT (1 May 2025) and the ~330 LMT over-buffer are DFPD/FCI/press; the
~₹18,000 cr excess-carry applies the ~₹5.6/kg/yr DFPD buffer rate to the excess (indicative — FCI's own
economic-cost carry components differ). Import bills are DGCI&S (oil ₹1.68 lakh cr verified; pulses
~₹31,000 cr). Recovery figures are the project's [consolidated-savings](consolidated-savings-by-crop.md)
(₹21,087 cr, additive/illustrative) and [OMSS-processing](omss-processing-returns.md) margins (verified
company financials). The **no-double-count** treatment (procurement sits inside the subsidy) is stated
explicitly so the ledger is not summed naively into a single inflated "total". **This is research
synthesis, not investment or fiscal advice** — the numbers are order-of-magnitude policy arithmetic, not
a budget.
