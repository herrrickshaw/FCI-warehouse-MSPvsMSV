# The 24 MSP crops as a domestic refined-oil source — cheaper than the ₹1.68 lakh-cr import bill?

*The edible-oil scenario ([DFPD](https://dfpd.gov.in/edible-oil-scenario/en)) asks whether India
can stop importing ~57% of its cooking oil. This report tests a sharper claim: that the crops the
government already sets an MSP for **contain the entire domestic oil-feedstock base** — and asks,
honestly, where procuring them is genuinely cheaper than importing, where it is only cheaper at the
system level, and where it is not cheaper at all. Import figures validated against DGCI&S TradeStat
EIDB (US$ Million). July 2026. Companion to [oilseed-msp-case.md](oilseed-msp-case.md),
[domestic-procurement-stability-layer.md](domestic-procurement-stability-layer.md) and
[rbo-savings-scenarios.md](rbo-savings-scenarios.md).*

## The import to beat — exact, from customs data

India's edible-oil import bill, from the DGCI&S TradeStat EIDB commodity-wise file (FY2025-26
provisional, US$ Million; [`data/edible_oil_import_parity_dgcis.csv`](../data/edible_oil_import_parity_dgcis.csv)):

| Oil (HS) | Import $mn | Volume LMT | Landed ₹/kg (CIF crude) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Crude palm (15111000)** | **9,053** | **81.8** | **96** |
| Crude soybean (15071000) | 5,257 | 47.1 | 97 |
| Crude sunflower (15121110) | 3,255 | 26.8 | 106 |
| Soybean oil, edible | 950 | 6.5 | 127 |
| RBD palmolein + refined palm | 463 | 4.2 | 95–108 |
| Palm kernel + rape + misc | 291 | 1.6 | 100–180 |
| **TOTAL edible oils** | **19,268** | **168** | **~100 (wtd avg)** |

**US$ 19.3 bn — ₹1.68 lakh crore — for 168 LMT (16.8 MT) of oil, landing at a weighted-average
of ~₹100/kg as crude** (CIF, *before* the ~27.5% effective import duty and *before* the refiner's
margin). This ties out to three independent authorities: the DFPD/NITI figure of US$19.2 bn (FY24),
and the **[Solvent Extractors' Association (SEA)](https://seaofindia.com/)** oil-year total of
**16.3 MT worth ₹1.61 lakh cr (MY2024-25, up 22% on value, flat on volume)**. Palm alone is $9.1 bn
and 82 LMT — half the volume, one oil, almost entirely imported.

**The trend the SEA data adds:** palm imports *fell* from 9.02 to 7.58 MT and palm's share of the
import basket dropped **56% → 47%**, while soft oils (soybean, sunflower) rose to **53%**, with
soybean oil hitting a record **5.47 MT** ([SEA via Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/economy/news/edible-oil-imports-up-22-to-1-61-trillion-in-2024-25-volume-flat-sea-125111400969_1.html)).
This matters for the procurement case: the growing half of the import basket is exactly the **soft
oils India can crush domestically** (Tier B below) — soybean and sunflower — not the palm it cannot
grow at scale.

**The supply side, from DFPD.** The [DFPD edible-oil scenario](https://dfpd.gov.in/edible-oil-scenario/en)
puts India's **nine cultivated oilseeds at 40.99 MT (2025-26, 2nd Advance Estimates, 10.03.2026)** —
~5–6% of world output — and India *exports* 5.44 MT of oil-meals/oilseeds/minor oils (₹29,587 cr,
FY23-24). Crucially, DFPD's *own* taxonomy names the feedstock set this report is built on:
"Groundnut, mustard/rapeseed, sesame, safflower, linseed, niger seed … Soyabean and sunflower …
Coconut … **Among the non-conventional oils, rice bran oil and cottonseed oil are the most
important**." The department itself flags the two byproduct oils as the priority — the Tier-A case
below is the government's own stated logic.

**This ₹100/kg landed crude is the number every domestic-source claim must beat.** It is low
because palm is the cheapest vegetable oil on earth and India buys it at the bottom of the value
chain. Any honest "procure-instead-of-import" case has to say *against which oil, at what price.*

## The 24 MSP crops already span India's entire oil-feedstock base

The striking fact the DFPD scenario understates: the MSP basket is not adjacent to the oil problem —
**it *is* the oil problem's supply side.** Every domestic edible-oil stream traces to a crop the
government already announces a floor price for:

| MSP crop | Oil stream | Yield | Domestic oil today | Is it a palm price-competitor? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Groundnut** (₹7,263/qtl) | Groundnut oil | ~40% | 12 LMT | No — premium (~₹200/kg) |
| **Rapeseed-mustard** (₹6,200) | Mustard oil | ~38% | **42 LMT (India's #1 indigenous oil)** | No — preference premium (~₹136) |
| **Soybean** (₹5,328) | Soybean oil | ~18% | 20 LMT | Near-parity, cake-dependent |
| **Sunflower seed** | Sunflower oil | ~40% | 3 LMT | No — output tiny |
| **Sesamum / niger / safflower** | Specialty oils | ~40–50% | ~few LMT | No — niche premium |
| **Copra** (coconut) | Coconut oil | ~63% | 5 LMT | No — regional premium |
| **Cotton** (MSP crop) → cottonseed | **Cottonseed oil** | ~13% of seed | 13 LMT | **Yes — byproduct, blend-grade** |
| **Paddy** (procured 37%) → rice bran | **Rice bran oil** | ~16% of bran | 10 LMT | **Yes — byproduct, ₹60/kg ex-mill** |
| **Maize** | Corn oil (wet-mill co-product) | small | niche | Byproduct |

Seven of the CACP's oilseeds are *direct* oil crops; copra is an oil crop; and three of the biggest
cereals/fibres the state *already buys or grows* — **paddy, cotton, maize — throw off oil as a
byproduct.** India's domestic oil output of ~122 LMT (44% of demand) comes almost entirely from
this MSP-crop set. The feedstock question is therefore not "can India grow oil crops" — it already
does, at MSP — it is **"is the state capturing that oil, or importing the shortfall while giving the
domestic byproduct away?"**

## The price test — three honest tiers

Whether procuring an MSP crop yields oil *cheaper than the ₹100/kg import* depends entirely on
which crop. Overclaiming a blanket "yes" would be wrong and would discredit the case. The truth is
three-tiered:

### Tier A — genuinely cheaper per kg: the byproduct oils (paddy & cotton)

This is where "MSP crop → cheaper oil than import" is **literally true**, because the feedstock
cost is ~zero — the primary crop (paddy, cotton) already pays for itself, and the oil rides free on
the byproduct:

- **Rice bran oil** — bran is the milling byproduct of the ~130 MT of paddy India procures and
  crushes. RBO lands **ex-mill at ~₹60/kg against an import parity of ~₹105/kg — a ₹45/kg edge**
  ([consolidated-savings-by-crop.md](consolidated-savings-by-crop.md); ~₹7,544 cr potential).
  India is already the **world's #1 rice-bran-oil producer** — and under the Custom-Milled-Rice
  system, hands that bran to millers *for free*, the ~₹7,650 cr/yr byproduct giveaway
  ([omss-processing-returns.md](omss-processing-returns.md)). The single sharpest "cheaper than
  import" oil in India is one the state *already owns the feedstock for and gives away.*
- **Cottonseed oil** — byproduct of cotton (an MSP crop); crush margin plus the oil-cake credit put
  it below import parity for blend/vanaspati grade (~₹1,975 cr potential).

**Tier A is the answer to the user's question in its strict sense: yes — procuring/retaining the
oil byproducts of paddy and cotton is a cheaper source of refined oil than importing, by ~₹45/kg,
and NMEO-OS itself names rice bran, cottonseed and coconut as collection-efficiency pillars.**

**And rice bran oil is not just cheaper — it is the *better* oil.** The case for capturing it is
stronger than pure cost, because RBO carries a health-and-cooking premium that palm does not
(International Association of Rice Bran Oil, *3rd International Conference on Rice Bran Oil*, Tokyo
2016 — with SEA of India on the organizing council;
[Shirai & Tsushita, *Am J Lifestyle Medicine* 2024](https://doi.org/10.1177/15598276241234012)):

- **Functional micronutrients palm lacks.** RBO is uniquely rich in **γ-oryzanol, phytosterols and
  vitamin E (tocopherols *and* tocotrienols)** — the fraction the project's byproduct tree values
  at ₹120,000–200,000/tonne as a separate nutraceutical stream
  ([consolidated-savings-by-crop.md](consolidated-savings-by-crop.md)). IARBO's stated physiological
  effects: **lowers blood cholesterol and triglycerides and improves circulation.**
- **A cooking oil built for Indian kitchens.** High oxidative stability at frying temperature,
  neutral smell/taste, and long shelf life — the reason it is the fastest-growing health-positioned
  oil in urban India, and why deep-frying HoReCa demand prefers it (232 °C smoke point).
- **Non-GMO, allergen-free, domestically made** — against a palm/soy import basket that is largely
  GMO-sourced and entirely foreign. India is already the **world's #1 RBO producer**; global RBO
  output is only ~1.5 MT, so India's byproduct base is a genuine strategic asset, not a niche.
- **The longevity frame.** Japan — the reference case for diet-and-longevity — built its
  post-war health gains on a plant-dominant, low-lipid diet and has historically prized rice-derived
  nutrition (rice bran itself cured India-and-Japan's 19th-century beriberi epidemics); RBO sits
  squarely in that tradition. *(Nutritional-research claims from the cited sources; not medical or
  dietary advice.)*

So the Tier-A logic compounds: rice bran oil is **simultaneously the cheapest domestic oil to
produce (₹60 vs ₹105 parity), the healthiest to consume, and the one India already leads the world
in** — and the state gives its feedstock away for free. If any single line in the edible-oil file
should change, it is this one.

### Tier B — at parity: soybean, sunflower (price-neutral, system-positive)

Domestic crush of soybean/sunflower lands *near* the import price, not below it — India imports
these precisely because global soy/sunflower is cheap. At MSP-supported seed (soybean ₹5,328/qtl),
oil crushes to ~₹110–140/kg depending on the oil-cake credit — roughly import parity, sometimes
above. So procurement here is **price-neutral at retail** but still positive at the system level
(forex, cake, strategic — §4). It cuts the import *volume*, not the *price*.

### Tier C — dearer: mustard, groundnut (premium oils, a production lever not a price lever)

Mustard oil runs ~₹136/kg ex-mill and groundnut ~₹200/kg — **above** palm's ₹96. These are
preference-led premium oils (North/East mustard, West/South groundnut), not palm substitutes, and
mustard (~115 LMT seed) is *already* almost fully crushed, so procuring it adds ~zero new oil
in-year — it changes *who* crushes the same seed ([oilseeds-biodiesel-consumption.md](oilseeds-biodiesel-consumption.md)).
Procurement here is **not a price lever at all.** Its value is entirely as a *production catalyst*:
the assured floor is the one thing that de-risks a farmer's decision to plant more oilseed, which is
the only lever that closes the import gap over years ([oilseed-msp-case.md](oilseed-msp-case.md)).

## Why procurement is still "cheaper" even at parity — the system-level ledger

At Tier B parity, importing and domestic-crushing cost the same *per litre of oil at the port*. But
the port price is not the national cost. Five items the import price hides make domestic procurement
cheaper for the economy even when the retail oil price is a wash:

1. **Forex retained.** ₹1.68 lakh crore leaves the country for oil every year. Domestic crush keeps
   it onshore as farm income, crush wages, and mill margin — the same rupee, spent at home.
2. **The oil-cake (DOC) co-product the import doesn't carry.** Crushing a tonne of soybean yields
   ~180 kg oil **and ~800 kg de-oiled cake** — a high-protein feed India *already exports*. Mustard
   yields ~60% cake. Imported oil arrives with zero cake; domestic crush produces a second revenue
   stream (feed, aquaculture, export) that offsets 20–40% of the crush cost. This cake credit is
   exactly what pulls Tier-B economics to parity in the first place — and it accrues *only* to
   domestic crushing.
3. **The ~27.5% import-duty wedge.** The ₹100/kg landed price becomes ~₹125–130/kg after duty before
   it reaches a refiner — a wedge the consumer and the exchequer split. Domestic oil sits inside
   that wall.
4. **Strategic security.** 57% import dependence on a handful of suppliers (Indonesia/Malaysia palm,
   Russia/Ukraine sunflower, Argentina/Brazil soy) is a food-security exposure a domestically
   procured buffer directly reduces — the deficit-crop stability-layer argument
   ([domestic-procurement-stability-layer.md](domestic-procurement-stability-layer.md)).
5. **Price stabilisation & farm income.** A binding oilseed floor plus a buffer smooths the
   import-driven price swings that today make domestic oilseed farming a gamble — and raises the
   real farm income the whole project tracks (RFII).

**So the honest verdict: at the byproduct tier (paddy/cotton) procuring MSP crops is cheaper per kg;
at the oilseed tier it is cheaper for the *system* (forex + cake + strategic) even when it is not
cheaper at retail; and for the premium oils it is a production lever, not a price cut.** The mistake
the loose version of this argument makes is claiming domestic oilseeds will undercut ₹96/kg palm at
the shelf. They won't. What they will do is cut the *volume* imported and keep the money, the cake,
and the security at home.

## The import-substitution savings estimate

Putting numbers to the tiers — what FCI/CMR stock reutilisation and oilseed procurement can actually
save against the ₹1.68 lakh-cr import bill:

| Tier | Stream | Mechanism | Vol (LMT) | ₹/kg edge | Savings ₹cr/yr | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Rice bran oil** | capture CMR bran (state owns the feedstock) | 7.7 | 45 | **7,544** | High |
| **A** | **Cottonseed oil** | crush margin + oil-cake, from an MSP crop | 2.0 | ~10 | **1,975** | Med-High |
| **B** | Soy/sunflower crush | buffer oilseed → domestic crush; cake credit + forex | at parity | system | ~1,500 | Directional |
| | **Capturable now (Tier A + B system)** | | | | **~₹11,000 cr/yr** | |
| **C** | Mustard/groundnut | production growth (NMEO-OS 39→69.7 MT) | multi-yr | premium | *ceiling below* | Lever |

**Two honest headline numbers:**

1. **~₹11,000 cr/yr is capturable now** — dominated by rice-bran-oil capture (₹7,544 cr), on
   feedstock the state *already procures and currently gives away*. This is real, per-kg-cheaper,
   and needs no new acreage — only a change in how CMR bran is treated. It is ~6.5% of the total
   oil import bill from byproduct oils alone.
2. **~₹84,000 cr is the production-lever ceiling** — if NMEO-OS's oilseed target lands and domestic
   oil output closes even *half* the 168-LMT import gap (~84 LMT × ~₹100/kg), that volume of imports
   is replaced over 5–7 years. This is the *volume/self-sufficiency* prize, not an in-year saving,
   and it depends on the oilseed MSV binding (next section). It is a ceiling, quoted as such.

The gap between the two — ₹11,000 cr you can bank now vs ₹84,000 cr you can build toward — is the
whole strategic point: **start with the byproduct oil the state already holds, use the proceeds and
the processing base to fund the production lever that closes the rest.**

## The PDS comparison — and why One Nation One Ration Card makes domestic RBO the natural PDS oil

Set the oil economics against what the state already sells through the ration system:

| PDS commodity | What the beneficiary pays | What it costs the state | Sourced from |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Rice (NFSA/PMGKAY)** | **₹0 — free** since Jan 2023 | ~₹40/kg economic cost (GoI) | domestic procurement |
| **Wheat (NFSA/PMGKAY)** | **₹0 — free** | ~₹27/kg economic cost (GoI) | domestic procurement |
| **PDS edible oil** (TN, AP etc., a *state* special-PDS item) | **~₹25/litre** | **subsidy risen ₹20→₹65/litre (2007→Jun 2024)** | **imported palmolein** |

The contrast is the whole point. **India procures grain domestically and gives it away free; but the
one PDS item it does *not* source at home — edible oil — is an imported palmolein the state
subsidises by ~₹65/litre.** Tamil Nadu's special-PDS tur-dal + palmolein bill alone is **~₹3,800 cr
for 2024-25** ([sources below]). The state is subsidising an *import*, when the paddy it mills for
that same free PDS rice throws off the bran to make a **cheaper, healthier domestic oil (RBO ₹60/kg
ex-mill vs the ~₹95–105/kg palmolein it imports).**

**The loop closes on itself:** FCI/state agencies mill paddy → free PDS rice → **bran byproduct →
rice bran oil → back into the PDS as the edible-oil ration.** Import substituted at both ends, the
₹45/kg edge banked against the subsidy bill, and the healthiest mass oil (γ-oryzanol, tocotrienols)
put on the ration shelf — from feedstock the PDS operation *already generates and currently gives
away.*

### Why One Nation One Ration Card is the enabler — added to its benefits

ONORC's usual benefits are stated in migrant-welfare terms (portability — collect your ration
anywhere on one card). But it also does something this thesis needs: **it turns the PDS into a
single, deduplicated, portable national demand signal** — and that is exactly the *contractable
captive offtake* a domestic-RBO-for-PDS supply chain requires:

1. **A unified ~81-crore addressable offtake.** ONORC standardises entitlements into one national
   system, so a domestic PDS edible oil (RBO) would have a nationwide, predictable demand backbone —
   not 36 fragmented state schemes. That scale is what justifies building domestic RBO/fortification
   capacity against a *contract*, the demand certainty the whole [markets case](demand-and-exports-highmargin.md)
   is built on.
2. **Deduplication tightens the contract.** Aadhaar-seeded ONORC removed crores of duplicate/bogus
   cards — so the demand estimate a supplier contracts against is *real*, not inflated. A cleaner
   denominator makes a state RBO-offtake agreement bankable.
3. **Portability enables a national fortified-oil standard.** Because the entitlement moves with the
   beneficiary, PDS oil can be a *single national fortified product* rather than state-specific SKUs
   — and RBO is a natural fortification carrier (already Vitamin-E rich; a Vit-A/D fortified PDS-RBO
   rides the [FRK fortification precedent](demand-and-exports-highmargin.md)). ONORC is the delivery
   rail; domestic fortified RBO is the ideal cargo.
4. **It converts a subsidy-on-imports into a subsidy-on-domestic-value.** Every litre of PDS oil
   shifted from imported palmolein to domestic RBO keeps the ₹65/litre subsidy circulating as farm
   income, milling wages and forex retained — the same rupee, spent at home — while the portable
   national system makes that shift administrable at scale.

**Honest caveats:** PDS edible oil is today a *state* special-PDS item (Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh,
etc.), not a central NFSA entitlement — making it national is a policy-and-fiscal choice, not a given.
And total domestic RBO output (~10 LMT) cannot alone supply a full national PDS oil ration yet; RBO
would substitute the *premium/health* slice and the states already running oil-PDS, with cottonseed
and the oilseed production lever filling the rest over time. The ONORC point is directional: it makes
domestic PDS oil *administrable and contractable at national scale* — it does not by itself create the
supply.

## The mechanism: MSP → a binding MSV as the production catalyst

The reason domestic oil output is stuck at 44% of demand is not agronomy — it is that the oilseed
MSP **doesn't bind.** Paddy/wheat are procured at 23–37% of output reliably; mustard/groundnut at
8–12% in good years and ~0 in bad ones ([oilseed-msp-case.md](oilseed-msp-case.md)). A farmer
weighing oilseed against wheat sees the same MSP promise but a *credible* one for wheat and a
*hollow* one for oil. That asymmetry is why oilseed acreage stalls and imports deepen.

The fix is the project's core design: a **capped, pre-announced MSV** (a volume guarantee,
θ ≈ 0.10–0.15 of output) that NAFED/NCCF actually execute for oilseeds — enough to make the floor
*real* without the open-ended fiscal exposure of unlimited procurement. That is what turns the MSP
notification into a production signal, and production growth (NMEO-OS target **39 → 69.7 MT by
2030-31**) is what actually replaces imported oil. **Procurement is not the oil lever directly — it
is the lever on the lever.**

## Recommendations

1. **Stop giving away the cheapest domestic oil.** Rice bran is a Tier-A, genuinely-below-import oil
   the state already owns the feedstock for and hands to millers free. Capturing bran value under
   CMR — a bran levy or a state rice-bran-oil offtake — is the fastest ₹/kg win in the whole
   edible-oil file (~₹7,544 cr; [rbo-savings-scenarios.md](rbo-savings-scenarios.md)).
2. **Make the oilseed MSV bind — for production, not in-year price.** Groundnut and mustard MSV,
   executed, is the production catalyst NMEO-OS needs; sell it honestly as a *volume/self-sufficiency*
   lever over 5–7 years, never as a promise of cheaper cooking oil next year.
3. **Site crush + refining on the procured feedstock, bank the cake.** The DOC co-product is what
   makes domestic soybean/sunflower competitive — capture it, don't import the finished oil that
   carries no cake.
4. **Price the comparison against palm, in public.** Any procurement case must state the ₹96/kg palm
   benchmark and locate itself in Tier A/B/C — the credibility of the whole MSV argument depends on
   not overclaiming at Tier C.
